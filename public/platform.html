What it handles

The administrative weight of a training programme, carried by the software.

Every part of this was written because a fellowship needed it that week — not as a feature list drawn up in advance.

01

Teaching schedule

Sessions, topics and presenters across the academic year. Drafts are assigned in bulk, reviewed, then published; swaps between presenters go through the portal rather than a reply-all thread.

02

Clinic rotations

Fellow templates, rotation grids and per-site coverage, with away dates and vacation requests folded into the same calendar the schedule is generated from.

03

Case log & competencies

Fellows log studies with nerves, muscles and diagnoses recorded as structured fields, so progress against competency targets is counted rather than estimated.

04

Feedback & evaluations

Post-session feedback and quarterly evaluations are requested automatically, chased automatically, and collected where the director can actually read them.

05

Handbook & library

The programme handbook, session materials and a reading list, versioned and kept where trainees will look for them instead of in an email attachment.

06

Electrodiagnostic toolkit

A 3D nerve and muscle atlas, a needle-EMG and NCS reference, a searchable diagnostic testing directory, and a literature digest. Enabled per programme.

How programmes sit side by side

Your programme's records are yours alone.

A fellowship's case logs and feedback are among the most sensitive records it keeps. Separation is enforced in the database itself, not by a filter the application remembers to apply.

  • Records

    Every row belongs to one programme. Cases, feedback, evaluations, schedules, attendance and trades are readable only from inside the programme that created them.

  • People

    Roles are per programme. Someone may belong to two fellowships with a different role in each, and sees each one separately.

  • Directors

    A director governs their own programme. They appoint their supervisors and fellows, and cannot reach another programme's records.

  • Platform

    Administration is walled off from clinical content. The account that creates programmes and enables tools does not get a window into anyone's cases.

Getting started

Four steps, and the programme is yours to run.

  1. Your programme is created

    A dedicated fellowship space, with its own name, its own people and its own records.

  2. You appoint your director

    From there the programme is self-governing: the director adds supervisors, fellows and administrative assistants.

  3. You choose your tools

    The electrodiagnostic toolkit is enabled item by item, so a programme runs with what it uses and nothing more.

  4. Your fellows sign in

    One address for everyone — and a sign-in button you can put on your own fellowship's website.
